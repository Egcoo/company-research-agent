# Passive Index And ETF Feeder Fund Research

## Three-Layer Framework

Analyze a passive index or ETF feeder fund in this order:

1. **Index exposure:** what the index owns and why it may earn or lose money.
2. **Vehicle quality:** how efficiently the fund and share class deliver that exposure.
3. **Current timing:** whether the underlying exposure offers acceptable risk-reward now.

Keep the three judgments separate in the report.

## Index Exposure

Verify the index universe, selection rules, weighting method, constituent cap, rebalancing schedule, sample count, concentration, and latest disclosed constituents or sector weights.

Explain real exposure through the benchmark and look-through holdings. For a thematic index, identify the dominant profit drivers and whether the label hides concentration in a few securities or sub-industries.

When judging valuation:

- Name the metric and aggregation method precisely.
- Explain distortions from losses, low earnings bases, financial stocks, or cyclical peaks when relevant.
- Use more than one valuation or earnings-quality lens when a single PE is unstable.
- Do not map an index valuation percentile mechanically to a fund NAV target; earnings changes can alter the ratio without a matching price move.

## Vehicle Quality

Verify the official benchmark, target ETF, minimum target-ETF allocation, current allocation, scale, fees, redemption rules, and material operational risks.

Evaluate both:

- **Tracking error or daily deviation:** the variability of tracking differences over a stated period.
- **Cumulative tracking difference:** fund return minus benchmark or target-ETF return over matching periods.

Do not call tracking quality good from one low quarterly tracking-error figure if longer-period tracking difference is material. Present both and avoid assigning causes such as fees, cash drag, subscriptions, or taxes unless the evidence supports the decomposition.

For ETF feeder funds, distinguish target-ETF trading liquidity from OTC subscription/redemption mechanics. Treat the index-fund manager mainly as an implementation and tracking function unless the mandate includes meaningful discretion.

## Performance And Risk

Prioritize comparisons in this order:

1. Official performance benchmark.
2. Target ETF, when applicable.
3. Underlying index.
4. Peers or broad indexes as optional context.

Use identical dates and return conventions. Show cumulative tracking difference for at least one recent and one longer period when available.

Describe concentration and downside using interpretable evidence: top-ten weight, dominant sub-industry weights, maximum drawdown, defined volatility, worst month or quarter, or another appropriate risk measure. Do not label a fund “low risk” or “highly liquid” from size alone.

## Share Classes

Compare A/C/I or other classes using actual access and holding assumptions. A simple break-even estimate is:

```text
break-even years ≈ effective one-time A-class purchase cost / annual C-class sales-service fee difference
```

Adjust for redemption fees, channel discounts, recurring purchases, and any other class-specific cost. State that the formula is an approximation; do not choose a class without the missing user inputs.

## Timing And Conditions

Base timing on the index's valuation definition, earnings or industry cycle, concentration, recent relative performance, and crowding or liquidity evidence when reliable.

Do not use an arbitrary 20% or 30% decline as an entry condition. A numerical decline is acceptable only when anchored to documented drawdown history, volatility, valuation normalization, or a reproducible scenario.

Write invalidation conditions for both layers:

- **Exposure invalidation:** earnings, cycle, policy, or valuation evidence breaks the index thesis.
- **Vehicle invalidation:** tracking difference deteriorates, costs or rules change, or disclosed implementation departs from the mandate.

## Completion Check

Before drafting, confirm that the report answers:

- Is the index exposure attractive?
- Is this fund an efficient way to obtain it?
- Is this share class appropriate under stated cost assumptions?
- Is the current timing attractive?
- What evidence would reverse each answer?
- What sector/theme does the index actually represent, and what is the sector's recent evidence-backed state?
- What are the material risk mechanisms and the specific indicators needed to monitor them?

Place these answers in the nine-part fund template rather than creating a separate passive-fund report structure. In `基金本身是否合格`, make the vehicle judgment from tracking error, cumulative tracking difference, fees, size, operational continuity, and target-ETF liquidity when applicable. Do not use manager stock-picking ability as a core criterion for a passive product.
