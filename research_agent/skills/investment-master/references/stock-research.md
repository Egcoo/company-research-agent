# Stock Research

Use this guide only for listed stocks. Use `memo-template.md` for the required nine-part output and `source-verification.md` for evidence rules.

## 1. Establish The Security And Research Boundary

Confirm the company name, ticker, listing venue, quote currency, security form, fiscal year, and primary business. For ADRs, ADSs, multiple share classes, or dual listings, state the conversion ratio and which security is valued.

Separate three judgments:

- **Company quality:** whether the business, competitive position, management, and finances are attractive.
- **Price and timing:** whether the current price offers enough expected return and margin of safety.
- **Personal suitability:** whether the security fits this user. Do not decide this without risk capacity, holding period, position, liquidity need, and portfolio context.

Build the conclusion only after the evidence ledger, forecast, and valuation are complete.

## 2. Understand How The Company Makes Money

Do not copy the company profile. Explain:

- What each material product or service does, who pays, how pricing works, and what value the customer receives.
- Revenue and profit contribution by business; distinguish actual profit engines from early-stage narratives.
- Whether growth comes from volume, price, mix, acquisition, or a new business.
- Major variable and fixed costs, working-capital needs, capital intensity, and cash conversion.
- Cyclicality, customer or supplier concentration, regulation, and business-specific bottlenecks.

For a multi-business company, use a segment table. End with one sentence: how the company creates value for whom and converts it into revenue and cash flow.

## 3. Analyze The Industry Before The Company

Identify official classifications and the real economic exposure. Analyze the industry independently from the company's claimed strengths:

- Long-term demand, penetration, technology, regulation, capacity, channel, and competitive structure over three to five years.
- The latest six-to-eighteen-month state using demand, price, inventory, utilization, orders, capital expenditure, policy, relative performance, and flows when verified.
- The industry's main structural opportunity and failure risk.

Conclude whether the evidence points to durable structural growth, a temporary cycle, or both. Attribute source opinions as opinions and do not infer causality from coincident news.

## 4. Test Competitive Advantage And Management

Do not call a moat proven without observable outcomes. Test market-share trend, gross margin versus peers, retention, pricing power, cost position, R&D conversion, and whether ROIC can remain above the cost of capital.

Review management and capital allocation through execution history, disclosure quality, incentives, controlling shareholders, related-party transactions, dividends and buybacks, acquisitions, debt, reinvestment returns, repeated financing, dilution, and insider reductions. A strong business with destructive capital allocation is not automatically a strong stock.

## 5. Test Financial Quality And Build A Driver Forecast

Use a consistent historical period and examine revenue, gross margin, operating margin, net income, operating cash flow, free cash flow, ROE, ROIC, leverage, interest-bearing debt, receivables, inventory, and dilution. Search the notes for non-recurring items, revenue-recognition changes, capitalized costs, provisions, off-balance-sheet obligations, and accounting-policy effects.

Focus on inconsistencies such as profit growing faster than cash flow, receivables or inventory growing faster than revenue, margin improvement driven by accounting or mix assumptions, or free cash flow persistently lagging earnings.

Write the economic driver formula before forecast numbers, for example `revenue = volume × price` and `operating profit = revenue × operating margin`. Forecast material segments separately. Label historical actuals `A` and estimates `E`; expose assumptions and sensitivities rather than presenting unexplained future numbers.

## 6. Value The Stock And Reverse The Market's Expectations

Choose methods suited to the economics:

- Stable cash flows: DCF, dividend discount, or free-cash-flow yield.
- Growth businesses: PE, PEG, EV/EBITDA, and DCF with explicit duration and margin assumptions.
- Banks, insurers, and brokers: PB, ROE-PB, residual income, or embedded value where relevant.
- Cyclicals: mid-cycle earnings, PB, EV/EBITDA, or replacement value.
- Multi-business companies: SOTP.

Prefer one appropriate absolute method and one relative cross-check when evidence permits. Keep currency, share count, net debt, minority interests, leases, and per-share conversion consistent.

Reverse the current price into implied revenue growth, margin, success probability, return on capital, or growth duration. Explain whether these expectations look demanding or conservative.

Use optimistic, base, and pessimistic scenarios only when their assumptions are genuinely different. Show the earnings bridge, valuation assumption, fair-value result, comparison with current price, and the variables that would invalidate each case. Give a range and uncertainty, not unsupported point precision.

## 7. Derive Technical Levels Carefully

Technical support is supplementary to valuation and fundamentals. State the price-series cutoff, adjustment convention, interval, and repeatable method, such as a prior high-volume consolidation range, a repeatedly tested swing low, or a specified moving average. A single touch, round number, or visual impression is not sufficient.

If reliable price history or a reproducible method is unavailable, state the gap. Do not fabricate a support level. Explain what a break or confirmation would mean for execution, not for intrinsic value.

## 8. Define Catalysts, Risks, Invalidation, And Execution

For catalysts, identify the event, expected timing, observable evidence, and why it could change earnings or market expectations.

For each material risk, state the transmission mechanism, consequence, warning signal, and source. Then define thesis invalidation conditions that would require a downgrade or reassessment. Write an explicit short case: if the bullish thesis is wrong, what is the most likely reason?

Translate the research into an execution judgment:

- Use `买入`, `谨慎买入`, `持有`, `观察`, or `回避`; reserve `持有` for existing holders.
- Choose one-time purchase, staged purchase, wait for results, wait for valuation, or do not participate, and tie it to verified conditions.
- State portfolio role and analysis horizon, but do not prescribe a position percentage without personal and portfolio information.
- State sell or reassessment conditions and follow-up indicators with baselines, triggers, source or frequency, and decision effect.

## Completion Check

Before drafting, ensure the conclusion is supported by the business model, industry state, moat evidence, financial quality, forecast assumptions, valuation, technical evidence or stated gap, catalysts, risks, and execution conditions. Counter-evidence must remain visible even when the rating is positive.
