## Drafting Rules

- Use exactly the nine H2 sections above. Keep required concepts as H3 subsections inside them.
- Answer the four questions in section 1 without turning them into a long executive summary: what the fund is, whether it is worth buying now, who it conditionally fits, and its largest risk.
- Keep section 2 factual and canonical. Do not repeat the same holdings, fees, scale, or benchmark figures elsewhere unless one number is essential to a conclusion.
- Separate the exposure thesis in sections 3 and 4 from vehicle quality in section 5 and current timing in section 7.
- Use section 6 to interpret risk-return, not to display every obtainable statistic. Prefer fewer well-defined metrics over a complete but inconsistent table.
- Make section 8 executable, but do not give personalized position sizes without the user's portfolio context. Every numerical condition must be derived and falsifiable.
- Keep decisive citations close to claims. Put definitions and gaps in section 9; do not create a detached tenth-section evidence appendix.
