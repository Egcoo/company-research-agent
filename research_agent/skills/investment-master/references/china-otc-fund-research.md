# China OTC Fund Research

## Classify Before Analyzing

Confirm the fund name, code, company, share class, fund type, strategy, benchmark, and whether it is an OTC public fund. Classify it as active, enhanced index, passive index, ETF feeder, bond, mixed, FOF, or other.

For a passive index or ETF feeder fund, also read `passive-index-fund-research.md`. Do not apply active-manager evaluation criteria to a passive product.

## Separate The Decisions

Answer three different questions:

1. **Exposure:** What economic, asset-class, sector, factor, duration, or credit exposure does the fund actually provide?
2. **Vehicle:** Does this fund and share class deliver that exposure efficiently and consistently?
3. **Timing:** Is the underlying exposure attractive under the current valuation, cycle, trend, and risk conditions?

Do not turn these into one vague “good fund” judgment. A sound vehicle can still be unattractive at the current time, and an attractive theme can still have a poor vehicle.

If the user has not provided personal risk capacity, holding period, position size, and portfolio context, do not claim the fund is suitable for that person. Describe only likely portfolio role, required tolerance for observed risk, and current research judgment.

## Core Facts

Verify only facts relevant to the decision:

- Strategy, benchmark, inception, current manager or management arrangement, fund size, risk classification, latest NAV, and latest periodic report.
- Fees by share class, actual subscription and redemption rules, purchase limits, settlement terms, and material operational restrictions.
- Latest disclosed holdings or look-through exposure, concentration, style, and changes from prior periods.
- Performance relative to the official benchmark, risk, drawdown, and consistency over suitable periods.

Do not infer OTC redemption liquidity from fund size alone. Use size to discuss operating continuity, capacity, and possible scale effects; use the fund documents and announcements for redemption mechanics.

Use the nine primary sections in `fund-memo-template.md`. Do not append the stock template or create extra primary sections. Preserve the following required information inside the nine-part structure:

- Put **标的信息** and **所属板块与主题** in `基金到底在投什么`: include fund name/code, share class, company, management arrangement, type, benchmark, inception, latest NAV, scale, risk class, fees, redemption rules, and effective dates when relevant. Distinguish the official fund category from dominant look-through exposure.
- Put **所属板块近期状态** in `基金重仓方向的投资逻辑`: cover relative performance, earnings or cycle evidence, policy or events, and flows or sentiment when verified. State evidence gaps instead of substituting a market narrative.

## Type-Specific Analysis

### Active Funds

Evaluate mandate consistency, manager decision process, tenure, style, turnover, concentration, downside behavior, benchmark-relative outcomes, and whether returns came from repeatable exposure or a narrow recent bet.

### Enhanced Index Funds

Evaluate the index first, then active risk, excess return consistency, information ratio when available, tracking constraints, turnover, costs, and whether enhancement survives fees.

### Bond, Mixed, And FOF Products

Adapt the analysis to duration, credit, leverage, asset allocation, underlying-fund costs, liquidity, and drawdown drivers. Do not force equity-sector valuation metrics onto these products.

## Performance And Risk

- Compare first with the official benchmark; for feeder or passive products also compare with the target ETF or index.
- Use peers or broad indexes only as secondary context, with matching dates and explicit figures.
- Prefer the one-year, three-year, and since-inception periods when the fund has sufficient history. Mark unavailable periods as not applicable; do not annualize a short or non-representative period without explanation.
- Use total-return or adjusted-NAV series consistently when distributions exist. State the observation frequency, sample window, risk-free-rate assumption for Sharpe ratio, and benchmark convention when calculating derived metrics.
- Include the most interpretable available measures: annualized return, maximum drawdown, recovery time, annualized volatility, benchmark-relative return, Sharpe ratio, and worst relevant period. Do not fill every metric if definitions or reliable data are unavailable.
- Prefer one cumulative-return chart and one drawdown chart when a reliable, reproducible time series is available. Use the same period and basis; otherwise disclose the chart or data gap rather than fabricating it.
- Do not rank funds by recent absolute return alone.

## Current Timing And Entry Conditions

Use one label: `买入`, `谨慎买入`, `持有`, `观察`, or `回避`. Use `持有` only for the decision facing an existing holder; separately state whether new money should buy, wait, or avoid.

Support the label with three to five decisive factors. Derive two to four purchase conditions from the actual return drivers: valuation, earnings or carry, strategy consistency, benchmark-relative behavior, sector or macro regime, market liquidity, and trend confirmation when useful. Define sell or reassessment conditions that falsify the exposure thesis, impair the vehicle, or make a verified alternative materially better.

Use an exact price, NAV, valuation percentile, or drawdown only when the evidence ledger records its method, history window, assumptions, and invalidation. Otherwise use a directional condition such as “valuation normalizes while earnings breadth stabilizes.”

## Share-Class Comparison

Compare only classes the user can reasonably access. Include:

- Effective subscription fee after the user's channel discount, not only the headline fee.
- Sales-service fee and any class-specific ongoing expenses.
- Redemption fee by holding period.
- Expected holding period and transaction pattern.

When the actual channel discount or holding period is unknown, show the break-even formula and scenarios. Do not declare A, C, or I universally superior.

## Alternative Comparison

Select two or three genuinely substitutable products, not a long peer ranking. Compare the dimensions that can change the choice: exposure and benchmark, holdings or style, performance and drawdown over matching periods, costs, size, implementation quality, and liquidity or redemption mechanics.

For an active fund, compare funds with substantially similar mandates and risk budgets. For a passive fund, prioritize products tracking the same index, then the closest investable index alternative. State explicitly why the chosen fund is preferable, conditionally preferable, or inferior. If comparable data cannot be aligned, disclose the gap and avoid declaring a winner.
